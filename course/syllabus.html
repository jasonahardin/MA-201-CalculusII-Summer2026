    Course Content.

    This course is a continuation of MA-200 (Calculus I). The main areas of study include techniques of integration (integration by parts, trigonometric integrals, trigonometric substitution, partial fractions, improper integrals), applications of integration (volume, arc length, separable differential equations), sequences and series and their convergence, power series, and Taylor and Maclaurin series. Parametric equations and polar coordinates will be introduced as preparation for their use in multivariable calculus.

    Subsection Course Structure, Assignments, and Grading

    Course Structure.

    This is a blended asynchronous course which combines asynchronous online work and in-person exams. All direct learning of course material will be done independently using a series of instructor-created videos. This allows you to work on your own schedule and at your own pace throughout the summer session (while meeting deadlines) with the ability to go back and review material as needed. It is strongly encouraged you try to work on course material each day throughout the week. The course is broken up into 11 units, due on most Tuesdays and Fridays throughout the session; each unit is roughly equivalent to a full week of a normal semester. The typical unit will cover two major topics from calculus, and will consist of the following:
    🔗
    • A series of videos posted on MyOpenMath presenting the course content.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Two online homework sets, one for each topic, completed on MyOpenMath.
      🔗
      🔗
    • A written homework set covering all the material in the unit, scanned as a single PDF and submitted on MyOpenMath.
      🔗
      🔗
    • A timed closed-note online quiz administered on MyOpenMath will follow the even-numbered units.
      🔗
      🔗
    More details about these assessments can be found below in the appropriate sections; a schedule with due dates can be found at the end of the syllabus. Due to the amount of work required for the course, you are strongly encouraged to work on course material each day throughout the week, as much as possible. This will lead to a better understanding of the course material, and subsequently a better course grade. Despite the asynchronous nature of the course, you are welcome to reach out to the instructor at any time with questions. The asynchronicity is designed to provide flexibility on when you work on the course material, not to create a hindrance to your success.
    🔗
    🔗

    Deadlines and LatePasses.

    All assignments in the course will have strict deadlines that must be met to receive credit. To account for any unexpected emergencies or technical issues that may occur, we will utilize the LatePass feature in MyOpenMath. A LatePass can be redeemed for an automatic 48-hour extension on an assignment (some exclusions apply, listed below); to use a LatePass, click the ’Use LatePass’ button next to the appropriate assignment on MyOpenMath and the platform will automatically extend the due date for that assignment to 48 hours after the original due date. Each student will be given 15 LatePasses to use as they wish throughout the course, with the caveat that only one LatePass can be used on any single assignment. LatePasses cannot be used on an online homework set that you view in ’Practice’ mode prior to redeeming the LatePass. If circumstances require a more substantial extension, contact your instructor as soon as possible to discuss this possibility; documentation of the circumstances will be required.
    🔗
    🔗

    Content Videos.

    All course content will be presented using a series of instructor-created videos, posted to YouTube and available through MyOpenMath. The videos will provide all relevant definitions, technique descriptions, and completely worked-out examples.
    🔗
    🔗

    Online Practice Sets.

    There will be two online homework sets for each unit, due 11:59pm on Tuesdays and Fridays according to the schedule at the end of this syllabus. While the number of problems in each set will vary, your score will be considered as a percent and each set will be weighted equally. Your lowest two online homework scores will be dropped. The online homework is set up so you can try most problems two times, then the correct answer will be shown (there may be some questions in which you have only one try, this will be indicated in the homework set). If you miss a problem and want to improve your score, you can click the ’Try a similar problem’ link, which will give you a new question of the same type. You can continue working on versions of a question until you get it correct. You must earn at least 50% on an assignment for your score to count towards your grade; any set on which you earn less than 50% will be counted as a 0. Assignments will remain available in ’Practice’ mode after their due date to provide additional practice of concepts; new versions of problems can be generated and answered, but scores will not be saved. Be aware that opening an online homework assignment in ’Practice’ mode will disqualify you from being able to redeem a LatePass on that assignment!
    🔗
    Although you will be entering only answers, I encourage you to work out each problem carefully on paper. This will help you follow your reasoning when you are reviewing for quizzes/exams. I strongly encourage you to start each assignment right away so you will have ample time to ask questions about each assignment. The exercises are intended to both provide practice of concepts covered in the videos as well as extend these concepts beyond what is done in the videos - in particular, you should expect to find some of the exercises difficult. Many quiz/exam problems will be based on the homework exercises, including the more difficult ones, so you should aim to understand the underlying idea for each assigned exercise. You should expect to spend several hours each week working on homework.
    🔗
    If you are having trouble on a problem, you may use the ’Message Instructor’ link, which will allow you to send me a message through MyOpenMath; the specific problem you are working on will be automatically included in the message. I can then reply to the message with some guidance. These messages are internal to MyOpenMath and not email, so my reply will go to your messages when you log in to MyOpenMath.
    🔗
    🔗

    Written Homework.

    Each unit will include a written homework set posted on MyOpenMath, due by 11:59pm on Tuesdays and Fridays, according to the schedule. Solutions to all problems should be written up neatly, include all relevant work, and use proper notation, like the examples in the videos. While obtaining a correct answer is important, you may lose points even when an answer is correct if you did not provide adequate work or use proper notation in your solution. Every written homework set should be submitted to MyOpenMath as a single PDF. Homework submitted in multiple files, in a format other than a PDF, or submitted via email will not be graded. Your lowest written homework score will be dropped; the rest will be weighted equally.
    🔗
    🔗

    Online Quizzes.

    A timed, closed-note quiz in MyOpenMath will follow the even-numbered units, and will be due by 11:59pm on the following Tuesday/Friday along with the next unit; the quiz following Unit 10 will be due on Monday, June 29. Your lowest quiz score will be dropped; the rest will be weighted equally. You will have 60 minutes to take each quiz; once you start, you must finish within 60 minutes. Once you submit your quiz in MyOpenMath, you must take pictures of or scan all relevant work you performed and submit it on MyOpenMath. You must submit all work which supports your quiz answers within 20 minutes of completing your quiz; failure to do so will result in a score of 0 on that quiz. I reserve the right to deduct points from a quiz problem if it is not fully supported in your submitted work, even if the answer is correct. Although quizzes will be completed online and will not be monitored, quizzes are intended to be closed-note; any evidence of cheating will result in a 0 on that quiz.

    Subsubsection Course Policies

    Extra Credit.

    There will be no extra credit available in this course. If you are interested in raising your grade, take the time you would have spent on an extra credit assignment and spend it making sure you get more "regular credit" instead: master a topic you’ve found difficult, find exercises in the textbook to work on, spend a few extra hours studying, etc.
    🔗
    🔗

    Studying and Coursework.

    To succeed in this course, you will need to dedicate a significant amount of time to working on calculus. Mathematics is learned by doing, meaning it is important to work numerous problems to fully understand the concepts we cover. The assigned homework is the minimum amount you should be completing; if you feel that you don’t fully understand a concept, work extra problems from a textbook or ask me for some additional practice problems. This is a 4-credit summer course, which means there should be 8 learning hours per week (a fully face-to-face section of the course would be required to meet 8 hours per week). A general rule in all college courses is for each hour of class per week you spend a minimum of two hours outside of class on homework, studying, reading the text, etc. Since this course has 8 online learning hours per week, you should plan to spend an additional 16 hours (for a total of at least 24 hours) working on calculus per week. This will, of course, vary for every student.

    Academic Integrity and AI Usage.

    The WSU Mathematics Department does not tolerate academic dishonesty! For an explanation of what is considered "academic dishonesty" at WSU, please see the policy included in the 2025-2026 Undergraduate Catalog. All instructors in the WSU Mathematics Department are required to report all incidents of academic dishonesty observed, detected, or otherwise determined to have occurred to the WSU Academic Central File. WSU requires some penalty be imposed for every reported violation of the academic honesty policy. All incidents of academic dishonesty in this course will result in (at least) a score of 0 on that assignment; additional penalties will be imposed at the instructor’s discretion and/or as required by the Mathematics Department Academic Honesty Policy. In this course, instances of academic dishonesty will include (but are not limited to):
    🔗
    • Submitting any assignment you did not complete yourself, either work produced by a classmate/friend or from an online resource (such as an online calculator) or third party (such as Chegg, Course Hero, etc.).
      🔗
      🔗
    • Using course materials/notes, the internet, or a calculator/other device in any unauthorized way during an online quiz or an in-person exam.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Sharing the contents of a quiz with a classmate.
      🔗
      🔗
    Generative artificial intelligence (AI), such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, etc., should not be used in any way to complete graded assignments in this course. Any evidence of generative AI use on an assignment will be considered an academic honesty violation and will result in at least a 0 on that assignment.

    Subsection Student Learning Outcomes and LASC

    Student Learning Outcomes.

    After successfully completing this course, students will be able to ...
    🔗
    1. Apply different techniques of integration, including substitution, integration by parts, partial fractions, and trigonometric substitution.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Use the definite integral in a variety of applications, such as volumes of solids of revolution, arc length of curves, and separable differential equations.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Determine absolute and conditional convergence of series using a variety of methods including the integral test, comparison test, limit comparison test, alternating series test, ratio test, and root test.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Perform operations on power series to construct related power series.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Compute Taylor series of functions and their intervals of convergence.
      🔗
      🔗
